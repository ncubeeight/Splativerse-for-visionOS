<h2 id="about">A new perspective on photography.</h2>

Gaussian Splats are a compelling new means for photographers to capture spatial photographs using photogrammetry. In photogrammetry, many different perspectives of 3D scene can be assembled into a recreated holograph-like scene that can replicate into a virtual space, capturing the appearance of the original scene viewed from those original perspectives captured by the photographer.

Create your own Gaussian Splat captures using Scaniverse, Polycam or video capturing tools to be rendered to Gaussian Splats separately. If you’ve downloaded an existing ply or spz formatted Gaussian Splat, put them into a Vision Pro downloads folder to open them in Splativerse. There you can use the spatial navigator joystick to move around in the Immersive Space view of the Gaussian Splat from your local headset.

Once you’ve loaded your Scaniverse for the first time, the app will allow you to navigate through all your Gaussian Splats one at a time.

If this is your first time exploring Gaussian Splats, consider visit an online platform like [Arrival Space Splativerse](https://live.arrival.space/51106658_5465) to see how photographers have been using photogrammetry to create navigable 3D volumes in a social and collaborative context.

Splativerse app is designed to view captures you’ve created or downloaded previously from a photogrammetrist like Christopher Arnold, Carla Knoop, Mark Jeffcock or Michael Rubloff. We do not provide public galleries or shared spaces as part of the app. To learn more about Gaussian Splatting visit [RadianceFields.com](https://radiancefields.com).

Splativerse is open source. So cloning, forking and modifying the code to your own specifications is permitted under [Apache 2.0 license](https://github.com/ncubeeight/Splativerse-for-visionOS/blob/main/LICENSE).
